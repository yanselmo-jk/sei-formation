SEI Formation · Methodology Briefing

Finding Every Reaction

This project stopped being “calculate the SEI of EC/EMC” and became build a reaction-discovery pipeline that transfers to electrolytes nobody has studied. That single change moved the centre of gravity from barriers to search — and then moved the bottleneck straight back onto barriers.

§1

The shape of the problem

The pipeline is a sequence, and the sequence encodes a real dependency: each stage consumes the previous stage’s output and cannot be run out of order.

S1reduction
Which species accepts an electron first Cluster + implicit solvent across the board, with AIMD demoted to a diagnostic. Produces the radical seeds everything downstream starts from.
S2discovery
What those radicals go on to form — the body of the project Compute-heavy, human-light. Splits into an append-only discovery ledger (for “have we found enough?”) and a working network (for “what matters?”).
S3barriers
Attaching a rate to each discovered reaction Human-heavy: ~0.31 h of human review per transition state. A few hundred explicit TS at most; everything else must be covered by a surrogate.
S4kinetics
Composition, thickness, dominant pathway, sensitivity 0-D stiff ODE with an implicit integrator — not kMC. Precipitation is a reversible pair with a computed solubility product, so no arbitrary cutoff is needed.

The two highlighted stages carry the project. They are also where its two scarce resources sit — and they are different resources.

The asymmetry that drives every allocation decision

S3 is human-intensive and S2 is compute-intensive. Compute is projected to leave 58 % of the envelope unspent; human time is what runs out. ADR-014 — moving weight from S3 to S2
§2

S2 — how reactions are discovered

The requirement is not “find the reactions of this electrolyte”. It is “find them in a way that still works on an electrolyte we have never seen”. That constraint decides almost every design choice below.

2.1 The transformation grammar is composition-agnostic

Search boundaries are not set by generation count or an energy cutoff — those require already knowing the answer, so they do not transfer. The permitted moves mention no elements at all:

break m bonds / form n bonds     with   m + n ≤ 4
charge change  Δq ∈ {−1, 0, +1}
spin change    (radical ↔ closed shell)
association / dissociation

Because no element list appears, the same grammar runs unchanged on a salt or solvent the pipeline has never encountered. Transferability is guaranteed at the grammar level rather than promised at the end.

2.2 Three engines — and the reason is statistical, not coverage

EngineMechanismEnergy modelSearch modePrincipal blind spot
engine-E1 Fragment principal molecules → recombine into a species pool → enumerate every stoichiometrically balanced reaction → filter → run many kMC trajectories and keep what fires thermo DFTdeterministic Computes no barriers. Judges by ΔG, so thermodynamically downhill but kinetically forbidden reactions pass straight through. Cannot reach molecules outside the pool — LEDC yes, SEI polymers no.
engine-E2 Graph-edit elementary steps → double-ended GSM at GFN2-xTB level → DFT refinement → IRC to verify the connection GFN2-xTBgraph edit + opt Best-in-class for gases, because it finds steps that actually have barriers. Bimolecular condensation (LEDC needs 3–4 generations) is lost to combinatorial explosion.
engine-E3 Inject an excess electron, run biased/high-temperature MD, extract events from bond-graph changes ReaxFF / MLIPstochastic MD Most MLIPs have no charge-transfer physics; high-T bias distorts branching ratios; rare bimolecular events at low concentration are effectively never sampled — which is exactly where the minor gas channels live.

The third axis is currently not stood up: ReaxFF was excluded, then PM7, then MOPAC — which removed its substance. The needed independence was re-derived as different random numbers rather than different physics, with Gaussian’s ADMP pre-registered as the fallback and an explicit trigger rule written down in advance.

2.3 Why one engine can never be enough

“Have we searched enough?” is answered with species-richness estimators borrowed from ecology — the same mathematics as “how many species are in this forest?”, which is answer-independent and therefore transfers.

Good–Turing coverage   Ĉ = 1 − f₁/N            f₁ = species seen exactly once
Chao1 richness         Ŝ = S_obs + f₁²/(2f₂)   f₂ = species seen exactly twice
stop when              Ĉ ≥ 0.95   AND   (Ŝ − S_obs)/S_obs < 0.05      [provisional]

The limit, stated by its own author rather than discovered later

Ĉ and Chao1 measure the coverage of the distribution the sampler samples — not the coverage of chemical truth. If an engine is structurally blind to a class, that class appears in neither f₁ nor f₂, so Ĉ reads high while reality is missed. proposer, ADR-015

That limitation is what forces capture–recapture, and it inverts the engine selection criterion.

Lincoln–Petersen    N̂ = |S_A| · |S_B| / |S_A ∩ S_B|

With one engine, completeness is not estimable in principle.
⟹ engines are chosen for independent failure modes, not individual performance.

One implementation line is load-bearing for the whole statistical argument

engine-E2 must not apply a barrier cutoff during search. Record everything that converges and filter afterwards. The moment a cutoff sits in the search stage, E2’s capture becomes dependent on xTB energies and its failure mode correlates with E3’s. proposer §16.1 — coder constraint

The reasoning: E2’s capture is driven by enumeration (P(edit enumerated) × P(TS search converges)) while E3’s is driven by energy (f(barrier, T, sampling time)). If xTB puts a barrier absurdly high, E3 never sees the event but E2 still finds the TS — only with the wrong energy attached. The reaction is still captured. The genuinely correlated failure is not a wrong barrier but a wrong structure or topology — unphysical dissociation of Li-coordinated charged species, wrong P–F coordination number. That is the thing to test for.

2.4 The ledger and the working network are different objects

Discovery ledger — append-only Never pruned. Records, per entry: species or reaction, discovering engine, round, discovery temperature, timestamp, initial-condition hash.
  • Ĉ, Chao1 and capture–recapture are computed here and nowhere else
  • Metadata only — storage cost is effectively nil
snapshot · hash-pinned
Working network — aggressively pruned Levels L0–L3 cut hard. Microkinetics, DRC and S3 selection operate only on this side.
  • Ranking, flux, selection
  • Rebuilt from a pinned snapshot, never fed back into the ledger

Two clocks run independently — clock A for discovery until Ĉ converges, clock B for S3 waves on a pinned snapshot. They need no manual synchronisation: while discovery is still adding high-flux reactions the ranking keeps moving, which drops the stability metric and stalls S3 execution by itself.

Computing Ĉ after pruning contaminates f₁ and f₂, making the completeness number entirely meaningless. Without this separation, completeness estimation and pruning destroy each other on the same data structure. ADR-027 — the U-05 decision

2.5 Layer I and layer II are counted separately

layer I    chemistry inside engine-E1's enumeration grammar   → exhaustive, converges fast
layer II   chemistry outside our grammar                      → only other engines reach it

Stopping criteria are evaluated per layer. Summed, layer I’s fast convergence hides layer II’s under-exploration. If layer II misses the threshold the pipeline does not stop — it reports under-explored and declines to produce a number. Layer II is also precisely where the “electrolytes nobody has studied” requirement lives.

2.7 Protection is defined by physical property, never by name

The original rule protected paths to LiF, Li₂CO₃ and LEDC by name. An unstudied electrolyte has no known target species, so the rule was rewritten in terms the pipeline computes anyway.

RuleDefinitionWhy it transfers
P-1Observable class set by ΔGsolv threshold — volatile (DEMS) vs condensed (XPS)ΔGsolv is an SMD output we already compute, not prior knowledge
P-2Residence time τ = 1/ΣkoutThis is the definition of “a product”; no name required
P-3Articulation edges — removal disconnects a large downstream subnetworkPure topology
P-4High flux × high branching entropyComposition-independent generalisation of the earlier heuristic
P-5Value of InformationAlready answer-independent

Priority is lexicographic: Tier A = P-1 ∪ P-3 > Tier B = P-4 > Tier C = P-5 > Tier D = residual flux.

2.8 How transferability is actually demonstrated

TestProcedureStrength
test-E1 Blind rediscovery — switch off target-species protection and literature seeding, then ask where the seven known products rank under P-1…P-5. Pass = all in the top 10 %. Smoke test. Passing does not demonstrate transfer — it is the same chemistry.
test-E2 Leave-one-chemistry-out — tune on carbonates alone, then test whether P–F chemistry is discovered. LIBE’s FEC/VC/FSI/TFSI supply the hold-out for free. The only development-time test that actually simulates an unknown system.
test-E3 Pre-registered prediction — freeze the pipeline, apply it to an unseen electrolyte, record the prediction first, then compare with literature. Strongest. The freeze is the discipline: adjusting after seeing results is not a test.
§3

S3 — why it became the critical stage

Improving the search moved the constraint rather than removing it.

The bottleneck moves from discovery to barriers. Discovering 10⁵–10⁶ reactions when a few hundred DFT transition states is the ceiling means the real deliverable is not the network — it is the barrier surrogate. ADR-015 — recorded as the round’s principal finding

Without barriers the network is an unordered list. S4’s kinetics, the identification of dominant paths, and the predicted SEI composition all stand on top of it. This is carried as an explicit project risk: if the surrogate fails, the exhaustive search becomes meaningless.

Each stage of the funnel is cheap except the last, which is priced in human review rather than core-hours.

Why 80 is a different kind of loss

150 → 100 is a resolution loss; the answer can still be shown to be wrong. 100 → 80 is a loss of falsifiability. Eighty reactions chosen by point DRC without ensembling leave no instrument capable of revealing that the choice was wrong. A result still appears — but it is a restatement of assumptions, not a verified prediction. proposer §9.1

Below that line the project’s claim changes from “we predict” to “we present one scenario”. Li₂CO₃ and ROCO₂Li fall first, and for a structural reason: several paths converge on them, so their flux disperses and DRC scatters low. LiF survives because it comes through a single PF₆⁻ channel.

3.2 The budget unit is a reaction family, not a reaction

This is the move that buys human time without lowering review quality: 15–25 families × 3–5 anchors ≈ 50–100 transition states cover a large part of the network with defensible rules. A reaction is exempt from explicit TS only if all four conditions hold.

TestConditionRationale
A1≥ 3 explicit TS already exist in the familyMinimum sample to establish a correlation
A2Family BEP residual σ < 0.1 eV0.1 eV is ×50 in rate — looser than this makes the rule meaningless
A3ΔG lies inside the family’s span — interpolation, not extrapolationAn extrapolated correlation has no basis
A4Ensemble DRC |X_RC| < 0.1Being wrong by 0.2 eV would not move the observable

A1–A3 are insufficient without A4: however good the correlation, a rate-controlling step must be computed directly. The converse also holds — satisfying A4 makes A1–A3 tolerably loose.

3.3 The surrogate — and why BEP was rejected

A linear Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi form ΔG‡ = αΔG + β produces negative barriers for strongly exothermic reactions. The Marcus intrinsic-barrier decomposition is used instead.

ΔG‡ = ΔG‡₀ · ( 1 + ΔG/(4ΔG‡₀) )²      for |ΔG| < 4ΔG‡₀
    = 0                                for ΔG ≤ −4ΔG‡₀     (barrierless limit)
    = ΔG                               for ΔG ≥ +4ΔG‡₀     (fully endothermic limit)

ΔG is computed, not learned — it comes from species thermochemistry we already have.
Only ΔG‡₀ is learned — one parameter per reaction family.
  • ΔG‡ ≥ max(0, ΔG) holds structurally; BEP returns negative values.
  • Near ΔG ≈ 0 it reduces to slope ½, containing BEP as a special case.
  • Parameters per family drop from two (α, β) to one — decisive at n ≈ 300.
  • ΔG‡₀ is a far smoother function of structure than ΔG‡, so it transfers better.

Microscopic reversibility comes for free

ΔG‡₀ is shared between forward and reverse directions, so ΔG‡_fwd − ΔG‡_rev = ΔG_rxn holds by construction. A model predicting the two directions separately breaks that identity — and then the kinetics develop cyclic fluxes that are thermodynamically impossible.

The architecture is a hierarchical Bayesian model with partial pooling: ΔG‡₀(r) = μ_global + b_family[family(r)] + w·x(r), with b_family ~ Normal(0, τ²). Families rich in anchors keep their own estimate; sparse ones shrink toward the global mean; a brand-new family gets the prior and a wide uncertainty — which then steers active learning toward it. Transferability, uncertainty and acquisition all emerge from one structure.

Feature design begins with an exclusion list: element one-hots, functional-group SMARTS, species names and system-specific constants are forbidden — any one of them breaks transfer. What remains is defined for every element: bond dissociation free energies, xTB Fukui functions and partial charges, ΔΔGsolv, charge/spin change class, steric size.

The acceptance criterion, promoted to a project-wide principle

Perturb the surrogate’s predictions by the uncertainty it claims for itself and re-run the ensemble. If the conclusion does not flip, the surrogate is sufficient for this decision, however inaccurate it is in general. The question is not “is the surrogate accurate?” but “does the conclusion we are about to draw survive the surrogate’s error?” proposer §15.6 → ADR-018

The falsifier is precision@k — the fraction of the surrogate’s top k that is in DFT’s true top k — together with the robustness test above. Reactions handled by rule get a barrier_source flag and a correlated ±0.2 eV sweep that is promoted from a reported error bar to a gate: if nothing flips, rule treatment is justified and no TS budget is owed; if something flips, the specific reactions responsible are promoted to explicit TS.

3.4 Wave 1 is filled by target back-tracing, not by flux

Rolling S3 shortens the critical path by 3–4 weeks, but filling the first wave by flux would have been wrong: early network flux is biased toward shallow paths, structurally demoting the LEDC route (3–4 generations deep) and the minor gas channels. DFT budget spent early cannot be recovered, so wave 1 is filled by tracing back from the target species instead — same schedule gain, no loss of the paths that the validation axes depend on.

U-15 is the uncomfortable one

The transformation grammar is element-agnostic and transfers. The starting set does not. Which molecules go into the principal list is still a human decision, and for a genuinely unstudied electrolyte there is currently no principle for making it. Everything downstream inherits that choice.

§5

Where the pilot fits

The cluster pilot is not a warm-up. Its largest item — P1, 9,216 of 16,679 expected core-hours, roughly 55 % — is described in the package as “one transition state carried to completion; the only measurement of S3 TS unit cost.” Its P6 items measure κ, how many times slower this node is than our reference, and S, the thread count at which Gaussian stops scaling.

κ currently   [ESTIMATE] 3.4 – 6.8   a factor-of-two spread
consequence   19.7 weeks  vs  23.1 weeks (schedule death)

κ converts core-hours into wall-clock, so until it is measured, “we can afford a better level of theory” is a number without units. That is why the methodology decisions above stop where they do — the remaining choices are gated on a measurement, not on an argument.

Infrastructure results already in hand: 200 concurrent jobs on 200 distinct hosts with zero rejections, queue waits of 57–60 s at 1/4/16 nodes, and the 48-hour wall limit confirmed by direct query. The compute-availability axis is settled; the chemistry unit costs are not.
